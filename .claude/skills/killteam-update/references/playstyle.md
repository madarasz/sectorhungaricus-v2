# Play style (Markdown body)

The play style is the Markdown body after the closing `---` of the frontmatter. In the CMS it is the "Play style" field.

These rules come from the user's own reviewed texts and from the user's corrections. Follow them exactly.

For examples, read the body of the reviewed teams (the teams with a non-empty `reviewed` field), for example `angels-of-death`, `deathwatch`, `nemesis-claw` and `xv26-stealth-battlesuits`. Match their length and tone.

## Length

- One paragraph. No headings, lists or Markdown formatting.
- 3–5 sentences, 45–80 words in English. Fewer sentences are better than a sentence with no information.
- The Hungarian text has the same sentences with the same purpose. It can be a little longer.

## Sentence plan

1. **Lore (1 sentence).** One vivid sentence about who they are in the setting. Paraphrase the source. Do not copy it.
2. **Identity (1 sentence).** Faction, and the type of team: elite or horde, melee or ranged, roster style. Do not write the number of operatives ("eight operatives", "a team of six"). The `operatives` fields show it already.
3. **Why it is special (0–2 sentences, optional).** Concrete strength, weakness or matchup of the team, only if the source states it. Leave it out if you have nothing concrete.
4. **Signature ability (1 sentence).** Only the single most characteristic faction rule. Say what it lets the team do on the table, not how it works.

## Where to find the information in the team rules PDF

- **Lore sentence:** the first paragraph of the "Operatives" section (it starts with "OPERATIVES", followed by a short paragraph about the team), and the "Designer's commentary" section, if there is one. Many PDFs do not have a "Designer's commentary" section. If it is missing, use only the Operatives paragraph, and tell the user.
- **Signature ability:** the "Faction Rules" cards. Select the single rule that is most characteristic for the team.
- **General traits** (fast or slow, fragile or tough, melee or ranged): only from what the PDF says about the whole team, or from obvious stats of the whole team.

## Writing about abilities

- Tell the purpose and the effect, not the mechanics. Good: "they can act at unexpected moments, even after they have activated". Bad: "spend 1AP so another servitor performs a free 1AP Network Counteract within 2"".
- Mention a maximum of one ability. Do not list ploys, equipment or each operative's skills.
- No action point costs, dice values, token counts, keywords in capitals or rule step names (Ready step, Strategy phase).
- A distance or a simple condition is fine if it is the core of the ability (for example "not visible to enemies further than 3"").
- Basic game terms are fine: operative, turning point, counteract, obscured, concealed, cover, vantage, melee.
- Do not mention common limitations that many teams have (for example "once per turning point", "once per battle"). They are technical details and do not show what makes the team unique.

## No empty sentences

- Each sentence must give concrete information about this team only.
- Delete statements that are true for all teams. Bad: "This team plays unlike any other", "rewards players who plan ahead", "good timing is the key to victory". Kill Team is a strategic game, so all teams reward planning and timing.
- If you say that a team is unique or different, say how.
- If a characteristic is short (an adjective or two), add it to another sentence. Do not make a separate sentence for it. Good: "Tech-Priests lead a crew of slow servitors that are weak in melee and armed with heavy guns, flamers and repair tools."

## No guessed tactics

- Describe the team as a whole: its lore, its general traits and its faction rule.
- Do not describe the role or tactics of a single operative.
- Do not guess how the team or an operative plays from its abilities, weapons, ploys or stats. Only write a playstyle statement if the source says it about the team directly. Bad: "the Technoarcheologist slips away alone to grab objectives", "push deep into enemy territory" (from ploys).

## Language

- English: plain, correct grammar. Same tone as the reviewed texts.
- Hungarian: natural and fluent, not a word-by-word translation. Correct spelling, punctuation and agreement.
- Do not capitalize general words for factions, races and armies in any language: "chaos", "space marines", "humans", also as an adjective ("a chaos team", "egy káosz csapat"). Capitalize them only inside a proper name (team, unit, operative or rule name, for example Chaos Cult).
- Do not translate English proper nouns (team, faction, unit and operative names, e.g. Blades of Khaine, Adeptus Mechanicus, Tech-Priest, Dark Gods).
- Keep game terms in English in the Hungarian text (counteract, obscured, concealed, turning point, heavy). Use "operatív" for operative.
- Do not use the "-ek/-ok" plural suffix on an English proper noun that is already plural. Write "a Brood Brothers tagjai", not "a Brood Brother-ek".

## Mistakes that the user corrected before

- Texts of 140–180 words. The target is 45–80 words.
- Ability descriptions that list action point costs, tokens, dice or rule step names.
- Sentences that are true for every team: "plays unlike any other", "rewards players who plan ahead", "good timing is the key to victory".
- Common limitations used as content: "once per turning point".
- Guessed tactics from operative abilities or ploys.
- A separate sentence for one or two adjectives. Put them into another sentence.
- The number of operatives in the text ("an elite team of only eight operatives"). The site already shows it from the `operatives` fields.
